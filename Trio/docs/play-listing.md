# Play Store listing — compliance notes and draft copy

## Why this file exists

The report (§6) flags Google Play's **Health Content and Services policy**
(effective 28 August 2025, with a further enforcement phase in January 2026) as
a hard gate for anything marketed around ADHD:

- Every app must complete the **Health apps declaration** in Play Console.
- Apps making health claims (diagnosis, treatment, management of a condition)
  need regulatory clearance.
- Non-regulated health apps must carry a disclaimer that the app is **"not a
  medical device and does not diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent any medical
  condition"** — and under the January 2026 phase that disclaimer must appear
  in the **first paragraph** of the description or the update is rejected.
- The January phase also adds Medical Device labelling and **Organization
  verification (D-U-N-S number)**.

The strategic response the report recommends, and which this listing follows:
market around **productivity, organisation and neurodivergent-friendly design**,
never clinical treatment.

## Pre-submission checklist

- [ ] Complete the Health apps declaration in Play Console.
- [ ] Confirm the disclaimer is in the **first paragraph** of the store listing.
- [ ] Complete Organization verification (D-U-N-S) if publishing as an org.
- [ ] Review every screenshot and feature graphic for implied clinical claims.
- [ ] Confirm no copy claims to treat, reduce or manage ADHD symptoms.
- [ ] Data safety form: Trio stores tasks **on-device only**, no account, no
      analytics SDK, no third-party data sharing.

## Language rules for all marketing copy

| Never say | Say instead |
|---|---|
| "Treats ADHD" / "reduces symptoms" | "Designed for how ADHD brains work" |
| "Clinically proven" | "Built on published design research" |
| "Therapy" / "intervention" | "A to-do app" |
| "Cures procrastination" | "Makes starting easier" |

The report's academic caveat is the reason for this table: the evidence base for
digital ADHD interventions is real but **low quality** (Gabarron et al., *BMC
Psychiatry* 2025;25:414 — 26 systematic reviews, 34,442 participants), and 8 of
those 26 reviews (~30%) noted adverse effects. Efficacy claims are not
defensible and are not made.

## Draft listing

### App name
Trio — Today's three things

### Short description (80 char max)
> Three things. Capture in seconds, see progress, enjoy finishing.

### Full description

> Trio is not a medical device and does not diagnose, treat, cure, or prevent
> any medical condition. It is a to-do app designed around how ADHD brains
> actually work.
>
> **Three things. That's a full day.**
>
> Most task apps hand you a list of forty items and let the thirty-seven you
> didn't do stare back at you. Trio caps today at a handful. Everything else
> waits quietly in Later, where it isn't a reproach.
>
> **Capture before the thought escapes**
> Add a task from the home-screen widget, a launcher shortcut, or your voice —
> without opening the app. Type it the way you'd say it: "call the dentist
> tomorrow at 3pm" or "submit report every Friday at 5pm". Trio reads the time
> and keeps the task.
>
> **See where you are, at a glance**
> One screen. A progress ring that fills as you go. No backlog, no badge, no
> counter of things you owe.
>
> **Finishing should feel like something**
> Swipe a task away and you get a sound, a haptic and a small burst of
> confetti. It's the best moment in the app, and it's meant to be.
>
> **Forgiving by design**
> Unfinished tasks don't go red — they move quietly to Later overnight. Nothing
> is ever marked overdue. There are no streaks to break and nothing punishes you
> for a day off. "Not right now" is always one tap away.
>
> **Reminders that actually arrive**
> Android quietly kills scheduled notifications to save battery, and it's worse
> on Samsung, Xiaomi, OnePlus and Oppo devices. Trio uses exact alarms and walks
> you through the specific settings your phone needs — once, in about thirty
> seconds.
>
> Your tasks stay on your device. No account, no sign-up, no tracking.
